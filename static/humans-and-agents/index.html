How humans and agents talk

How work moves between people and AI agents, inside one company and between companies. Who is on each end, what carries the messages, who signs off, and what's still missing.

Evidence current to 18 October 2026 · 221 sources

More of my work now starts as a draft from an agent. A status update for a client. A reply to a supplier. A list of questions for a new vendor. More of what I receive gets read first by the other person's agent, which summarizes it, answers what it can, and flags the rest for them.

So on each end of a lot of business communication there is now a pair: a person and their agent. I wanted to know how that actually works today. Inside a company, where everyone shares one login and one set of rules. And between companies, where nobody does. This site is what I found.

What I found

Inside a company, most of the hard parts are being solved. Identity providers now register agents alongside employees, MCP connects agents to the company's tools, and approvals sit in the workflow. The gap inside is people using personal AI accounts that the company never sees.

Between companies, agent work still runs through people. Of 25 public deployments that cross a company line, only two put an AI agent on both sides, and neither commits anyone to anything. Everything with real volume is one company's agent dealing with another company's people, an agent paying a posted price, or agents drafting while people send.

One side always sets the limits. Walmart's negotiation agent works inside a ceiling Walmart chose, and the supplier's rep accepts or declines. None of the 25 deployments has two companies' agents comparing their limits with each other.

And outside of payments, there's no standard record of what was approved. Card payments are the one place where a company can check, from a signed record, what a named person on the other side authorized. Nothing like it exists for a contract term, a data release, or a change to an order.

    Two public cases of one company's agent working with another's. Elsewhere, agents trade documents through fixed rules.
